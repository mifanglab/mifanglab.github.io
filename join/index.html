# Join MI² Lab

Graduate studies · Research training · Collaboration

Build clinically grounded medical intelligence

We welcome students who want to connect rigorous computation with real medical questions. The lab combines foundational algorithm research, multimodal medical data, and translational collaborations with hospitals.

Contact Prof. ZhangHow to apply

# Why MI² Lab

International perspective

Training and research experience at TU/e, USC Keck School of Medicine, the Chinese Academy of Sciences, and Nanjing University.

Proven mentorship

Research published across TPAMI, TMI, Medical Image Analysis, JBHI, Pattern Recognition, MICCAI, and other leading venues.

Clinical connection

Work with medical images and clinical collaborators to turn algorithms into measurable biomarkers and decision support.

# Research Directions

01

Ophthalmic imaging AI

Fundus, OCT, OCTA, UWF, FA, and confocal microscopy; image enhancement, lesion and structure segmentation, foundation models, and multimodal large models.

02

Eye–heart–brain intelligence

Multimodal computation across organs, disease representation, clinically grounded reasoning, and medical agents for workflow support.

03

Vascular imaging and diagnosis

Retinal and neurovascular analysis, 2D/3D vessel reconstruction, geometric and topology-aware modeling, biomarkers, diagnosis, and treatment planning.

# Who We Are Looking For

  • Applicants for master’s, regular Ph.D., or direct Ph.D. programs at Nanjing University.
  • Background in computer science, biomedical engineering, medical imaging, clinical medicine, applied mathematics, or a related discipline.
  • Clear interest in medical imaging, computer vision, multimodal large models, medical agents, or medicine–engineering research.
  • Strong programming and mathematical foundations; publication or open-source experience is a plus.
  • Reliable, collaborative, proactive in communication, and ready for sustained research.

Also welcome

Research assistants, interns, visiting students, and collaborators from hospitals, imaging centers, and life-science research groups.
